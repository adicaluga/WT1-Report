Introduction

From May to August 2026, I worked as an AI Solutions Co-op Student at BWXT Canada, with my work term later extended through December. My main responsibility was to help digitize, automate, and AI enhance existing internal processes that had opportunities for improvement.

Through this report, I hope to show how my work term gave me experience applying software development to real engineering and manufacturing problems, while also introducing me to the challenges involved in designing software for real users and existing business processes.

About BWXT

BWXT Canada is a large engineering and manufacturing company involved in complex industrial and technical work, including the production of nuclear power generation equipment, CANDU fuel, and other nuclear components. Because of the scale and complexity of its operations, software plays a very important role in helping employees manage information and support internal processes.

Software is used throughout the company to support engineering, manufacturing, and internal business processes. My role focused on developing software solutions for some of these internal workflows.

All of the various different challenges and uncertainties throughout the project pushed me to learn rapidly and adapt as the work changed.

Job Description

My main project during the work term was the development of a greenfield software project to digitize and automate parts of the welder qualification process. The existing process requires information to be gathered and cross referenced from several different sources in different types of formats (papers, pdfs, spreadsheets). The goal of the project was to bring more of this information into one application and reduce the amount of manual work required from users.

I worked on every technical part of the application, this includes the frontend, backend, database, and overall software architecture. The application uses technologies such as React, TypeScript, FastAPI, Python, and PostgreSQL. One of the biggest obstacles was working with data that was not originally structured for use in an automated system. Much of the important information we needed existed in collections of documents rather than in a database designed around the process, so we had to improvise and find different ways to make the information usable.

It is now moving toward being hosted through Azure, and we are working toward adding AI functionality grounded in the application’s data. This would allow the system to assist users with tasks, as well as run in the background to identify discrepancies or changes in information.

Alongside the main project I also worked on a little side project using Microsoft Power Apps and Power Automate. I developed a simple interface for capturing and uploading photographs as part of an existing internal process. The application automatically applies a naming convention through Power Automate, uses a Python script to burn a timestamp into the image, and then sends it over to a sharepoint.

Some of the skills I used came from my Software Design course (CIS 3250). This helped with requirements gathering and planning the architecture of the main application. Most of my full stack development experience was developed on the job. I also became much more comfortable working directly with people across the company to understand what they needed and explain technical work in a way that made sense from a business perspective.

Conclusion

This work term has definitely given me a much better understanding of how software is developed in a real workspace. I improved my full stack development skills and learned how to work in a high stakes environment with changing requirements throughout the project.
